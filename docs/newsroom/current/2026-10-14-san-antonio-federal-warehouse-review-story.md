# San Antonio Sues DHS and ICE Over Environmental Review of East Side Detention Warehouse

**Published:** October 14, 2026

**Deck:** A newly filed federal lawsuit challenges environmental review for a proposed detention facility with capacity for up to 1,500 people. The complaint is contested, and no court ruling has been established.

## Introduction

San Antonio filed a federal lawsuit on October 13, 2026, over plans to convert an East Side commercial warehouse into a U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement detention facility. The city alleges that the Department of Homeland Security and ICE did not complete an adequate federal environmental review before advancing the project. The allegations are contained in a file-stamped federal complaint; they are not findings by a judge.

The case concerns a nearly 640,000-square-foot facility at 542 S.E. Loop 410 that could house up to 1,500 people. City officials seek judicial relief to stop implementation while environmental requirements are addressed. Federal authorities dispute the city's motives, and two members of San Antonio City Council have publicly opposed the lawsuit. The dispute raises concrete questions about agency procedures, utility capacity and how local government can challenge a federally controlled development.

## What the city actually filed

The complaint is dated October 13 and filed in the U.S. District Court for the Western District of Texas as case 5:26-cv-06542. It names the Department of Homeland Security, Immigration and Customs Enforcement and federal officials in their official capacities. The city invokes the National Environmental Policy Act, commonly called NEPA, and the Administrative Procedure Act, or APA. It requests declaratory and injunctive relief and seeks to set aside federal decisions involved in buying and converting the property.

A filed lawsuit does not automatically halt the project. A judge must decide whether any immediate restraint is warranted and, eventually, resolve the legal issues presented. The city's pleading states its position and desired remedies; it does not establish that an environmental violation occurred. At the time of this report, the reviewed sources did not establish a final ruling allowing the conversion to go forward or permanently blocking it.

## The warehouse, contracts and proposed capacity

The existing commercial property was designed for industrial storage and distribution rather than overnight detention. Published acquisition and procurement reporting places the warehouse purchase at approximately $66.1 million and an initial conversion award to GardaWorld Federal Services at about $17.3 million. The proposal has been described as accommodating as many as 1,500 detained people.

Those numbers should not be confused with operating expenditures or actual occupancy. Purchasing a warehouse does not demonstrate that the converted facility has opened, and the stated capacity is not a count of current detainees. Similarly, a federal contractor's initial award is different from a final project cost. The case is about federal decision-making in the conversion, including what environmental analysis should have occurred before commitments were made.
