# Texas Appeals Prison Air-Conditioning Order, Seeks Stay of 2029 Deadline

**Published:** October 14, 2026

**Deck:** An October 8 notice of appeal and stay request challenge a federal judgment requiring climate control across Texas prisons by the end of 2029. The filings do not themselves overturn the September ruling.

## Introduction

Texas prison officials have appealed a September federal judgment requiring air conditioning throughout the state's prison system by December 31, 2029. The state filed its notice of appeal and a separate request to suspend the judgment on October 8, 2026. Both court documents are available publicly. The move creates a new legal question about whether compliance must continue on the district court's timetable while the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit considers the case.

The Texas Department of Criminal Justice has long maintained that cooling its entire system is a priority, but it argues the court's schedule conflicts with the practical demands and costs of operating old and crowded facilities. Prisoners and their attorneys contend that long-term exposure to dangerous indoor heat violates constitutional protections. The existence of an appeal means a higher court will review the judgment; it does not mean that the state has already won a reversal or a stay.

## What the October 8 filings say

The official notice of appeal is filed as Document 360 in federal civil case 1:23-cv-01004-RP in the Western District of Texas. It states that TDCJ Executive Director Bobby Lumpkin, sued in his official capacity, is appealing the district court's September 22 final judgment to the Fifth Circuit. A separate filing, Document 361, requests a stay pending appeal, which would postpone the judgment's operative effect while the appellate litigation proceeds if the judge grants it.

A motion is not a ruling. The stay request is opposed, and the district court must evaluate it under the relevant legal standard. Readers should distinguish three procedural events: the September trial court judgment, the October notice of appeal, and any later decision granting or denying a stay. Only the last of those would establish whether the original timetable has been paused.

## What the September judgment required

U.S. District Judge Robert Pitman issued a final judgment in September after litigation over extreme heat in Texas prison units without mechanical air conditioning. The judge found that the conditions implicated the Eighth Amendment's ban on cruel and unusual punishment and that the agency's response was constitutionally insufficient. He ordered the prison system to develop and carry out a plan to provide climate control in state prisons by the end of 2029.

The ruling covered a statewide system, not one individual jail. Public reporting identifies 104 TDCJ prison facilities, many dating to periods when comprehensive cooling was not part of their original design. KeepTXRed separately covered the September ruling when it happened. The October appeal is a new development and is linked to that earlier report so readers can distinguish a trial-court order from later appellate proceedings.
