# KeepTXRed Free Image Sourcing Policy

Use this policy whenever a KeepTXRed article, evergreen page, election page, historical explainer, or other editorial surface needs a free-to-use image that is not being generated in-house.

## Approved search sources

Search these sources before falling back to generic web image search:

1. **Wikimedia Commons** — https://commons.wikimedia.org/
   - Strong first choice for public figures, government buildings, landmarks, historical subjects, maps, and archival images.
   - Check the individual file page. Commons files can use different licenses and can require creator credit, source credit, a license link, or share-alike compliance.

2. **The Portal to Texas History** — https://texashistory.unt.edu/
   - Excellent for Texas history, historic newspapers, maps, photographs, illustrations, buildings, towns, and archival material.
   - This is an approved *search source*, not a blanket free-commercial-use library. Check the individual item's "Citations, Rights, Re-Use" information and the collection-holding partner's rights statement before publishing on KeepTXRed.
   - Do not use an item commercially when rights are unclear, restricted, or limited to research/teaching/private study.

3. **PICRYL** — https://picryl.com/
   - Useful for public-domain and "no known copyright restrictions" historical material, especially U.S. government, military, archival, maps, posters, and older photographs.
   - Verify the original-source record when possible. "No known copyright restrictions" is not the same as a legal guarantee that no rights exist.

4. **Unsplash** — https://unsplash.com/
   - Useful for modern Texas landscapes, skylines, buildings, weather, infrastructure, agriculture, business, and non-event-specific editorial context.
   - Standard Unsplash images may be used commercially under the Unsplash License without required attribution, although credit is encouraged.
   - Avoid paid/Unsplash+ material unless its license has been separately confirmed for the intended use.
   - Do not use generic identifiable people to illustrate political, criminal, legal, health, or other sensitive stories in a way that could falsely imply they are involved.

5. **Pixabay** — https://pixabay.com/
   - Useful for royalty-free modern photography, illustrations, backgrounds, landscapes, infrastructure, and general Texas context.
   - Check the current Pixabay Content License and any item-specific third-party rights involving recognizable people, trademarks, property, or brands.
   - Avoid sponsored, premium, or externally licensed search results.

6. **OpenStreetMap** — https://www.openstreetmap.org/
   - Use for maps and geographic context, not as a general photo source.
   - Preserve required OpenStreetMap attribution and make the Open Database License terms available as required.
   - Do not assume that the public OpenStreetMap tile servers are a free bulk tile-hosting service. Follow current tile-usage rules or use an approved map provider/rendering path.

## Search priority

For exact people, exact places, historical events, government subjects, and named institutions:

1. Exact-subject Wikimedia Commons image with compatible rights
2. Exact-subject official/public-record image whose reuse rights are clear
3. Exact-subject Portal to Texas History or PICRYL item with verified compatible rights
4. Exact-subject Unsplash or Pixabay image when available and contextually accurate
5. Neutral contextual image only when the article clearly does not imply the image depicts the reported event
6. Generated image only when no suitable reusable real image is available and generation is appropriate under KeepTXRed's image-grounding rules

Prefer an exact image of the actual subject over a generic Texas image whenever licensing permits.

## News and politics safeguards

KeepTXRed is an editorial/news site, so image accuracy matters as much as licensing.

- Never present an unrelated stock photo as though it depicts a specific rally, hearing, press conference, crime scene, disaster, election event, or other dated event.
- Never use a look-alike or unrelated person as a substitute for a named public figure.
- For a named politician or candidate, prefer a correctly identified real photograph with verified reuse rights over a generated likeness.
- Do not imply endorsement by a person, campaign, agency, business, photographer, archive, or source.
- For sensitive stories, avoid stock photos containing identifiable private people unless the use is clearly non-deceptive and permitted.
- Historical images must be labeled or captioned in a way that does not imply they are contemporary.

## Required rights check before publishing

Before using any externally sourced image, record or verify:

- exact source page URL
- image creator/photographer when available
- original institution or collection when applicable
- license or rights status
- required attribution text
- required license link, if any
- whether commercial/editorial reuse is allowed
- whether recognizable people, trademarks, artwork, buildings, or other third-party rights create additional restrictions

If the rights status is unclear, **do not publish the image**. Search another approved source or use the generated-image pipeline when appropriate.

## Attribution

Even when attribution is optional, retain source metadata internally whenever practical. When attribution is required, display it in the article caption, image credit, source field, or other persistent editorial metadata that survives syndication and social sharing where feasible.

Examples:

- `Photo: Jane Smith / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0`
- `Courtesy of [collection-holding institution] via The Portal to Texas History`
- `Photo: Jane Smith / Unsplash`
- `Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors · ODbL`

## Google Images and general web search

Google Images, Bing Images, social media, news sites, campaign sites, and random webpages are discovery tools only. A search result thumbnail is never proof of permission. Follow the image to its original source and verify rights before downloading or publishing it.

Do not copy images from another news publisher simply because they appear in search results.

## Sponsored and premium results

Do not use an image whose apparent free availability actually routes to a paid stock provider, sponsored listing, subscription product, or premium tier unless KeepTXRed has explicitly acquired the necessary license.

## Editorial recordkeeping

Where the image pipeline or content model supports it, preserve:

- `image_source`
- `image_source_url`
- `image_creator`
- `image_license`
- `image_attribution`
- `image_rights_verified_at`

This policy applies to manual image selection, AI-assisted image research, backfills, evergreen content, election content, historical content, and future automated free-image search tooling.
