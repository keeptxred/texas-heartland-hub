# Texas Estates Code §253.002 — bounded will-revocation claim review

**Date:** October 29, 2026. **Module:** `src/data/law-topic-wills-probate-authority.ts`. **Status:** Partial, NOT full legal claim certification.

## Authoritative enacted text

[Current Texas Estates Code Chapter 253 §253.002](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/ES/htm/ES.253.htm) allows revocation of a written will, a clause or a devise only by (i) a later will/codicil/declaration in writing executed **with like formalities**, or (ii) testator's destruction/cancellation of the will, or causing it to be destroyed/canceled in the testator's presence. **§253.001** separately makes an attempt by a court to bar execution, codicil or revocation void. [Texas State Law Library](https://guides.sll.texas.gov/probate/probating-a-will) distinguishes will requirements and actual probate procedures.

## Scoped editorial effect

One paragraph, one key rule, FAQ, source citation and regression clarify that simply expressing oral wishes or revising an unsigned draft does not automatically revoke an executed will. The statute can permit a clause-only revocation; do not assert all revocation must cancel the whole will. Actual intent, document provenance, presumptions for a missing will and competing testamentary instruments are **not adjudicated by this generic article**.
