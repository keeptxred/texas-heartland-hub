# Texas residential security deposits — bounded statutory accounting review

**Date:** October 29, 2026. **Module:** `src/data/law-topic-landlord-tenant-authority.ts`. **Status:** Partial; no full-guide sign-off.

## Primary current law

[Texas Property Code Chapter 92, Subchapter C](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/PR/htm/PR.92.htm):

- **§92.103(a)** imposes the ordinary thirty-day refund requirement after surrender, conditioned by **§92.107** on receipt of a written forwarding address, without forfeiting the tenant's underlying claim merely for absent address under (b).
- **§92.104(a)-(c)** permits certain legally owed deductions, forbids normal-wear-and-tear deductions and ordinarily requires an itemized deduction description. **§92.104(c)(1)-(2)** has a narrow exception: tenant owes rent on surrender **and** there is no dispute about the amount due. A landlord alleging disputed rent does not automatically invoke this exception.
- **§92.1041** provides a presumption of timely accounting/refund on proper U.S.-mail placement and timely postmark; do not assert the tenant must physically receive mail on the last day to comply.
- **§92.109(a)-(d)** conditions multiple damages on bad-faith withholding, places the burden of proving reasonable retention on the landlord in a tenant's deposit action, and has a conditioned 30-day bad-faith presumption. Disputed charges alone do not establish bad faith.
- [Texas State Law Library deposit-refund guidance](https://guides.sll.texas.gov/landlord-tenant-law/security-deposit-refunds) confirms the statutory timing/forwarding-address distinction.
