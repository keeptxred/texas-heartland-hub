# Texas abortion law: SB 31 medical-emergency provisions, HB 7 drug enforcement and 2026 Weldon procedure — bounded no-change review

**Reviewed:** October 28–29, 2026. **Guide:** `src/data/law-topic-abortion-authority.ts` / `/laws/topic/abortion-law`. Public guide's original `updated: 2026-09-06` remains unchanged.

## Independent official authorities and selected verified assertions

1. [Texas Legislature — 89(R) SB 31 final enrolled summary](https://capitol.texas.gov/billlookup/BillSummary.aspx?Bill=SB31&LegSess=89R) identifies the **Life of the Mother Act**, **effective June 20, 2025**. It amended the Civil Practice and Remedies Code, Health and Safety Code and professional-regulation provisions to clarify reasonable-medical-judgment medical-emergency exceptions. The 2025 act clarifies that a physician may act before the pregnant patient's physical risk has already caused harmful effects; it is not a free-standing general exception for any pregnancy complication.
2. [Texas Health and Safety Code Chapter 170A, current official law](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/HS/htm/HS.170A.htm), specifically:
   - **§170A.002(c-1)–(c-2)** expressly says the physician need not wait until qualifying risk is *imminent*, physical impairment occurred, or damage resulted; a life-threatening physical condition can be **capable of causing death or potentially fatal**, not necessarily actively injuring the patient. Must still meet the statute's actual §170A.002(b)(2) circumstances.
   - **§170A.0021(b)–(d)** balances prescribed treatment and greater-risk exceptions, does not require delaying/altering/withholding treatment when doing so creates greater maternal death/major-bodily-function risk, but expressly **does not authorize an otherwise prohibited abortion**. The guide preserves that limitation and avoids advising patients on particular treatments.
   - **§170A.0022** includes removal of **ectopic pregnancies** and a deceased unborn child whose death resulted from **spontaneous abortion** within reasonable-medical-judgment treatment. The guide accurately avoids asserting either type of care is categorically prohibited by the general abortion framework.
3. [Health and Safety Code Chapter 171A, enacted by HB 7](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/HS/htm/HS.171A.htm) effective **December 4, 2025**: §171A.051 restricts enumerated Texas-linked abortion-inducing-drug manufacture, distribution, delivery, transportation, prescription and provision subject to stated exceptions. The enforcement subchapter creates a private **civil qui tam** cause of action rather than simply replacing existing criminal law with a new identical crime; it protects the pregnant woman's own conduct from these qui tam actions and contains medical and federal-law exclusions. The guide correctly distinguishes Chapter 170A, Chapter 171 and Chapter 171A and does not equate all conduct with identical sanctions.
4. [Texas Supreme Court — Weldon v. Lilith Fund, May 15, 2026 official order/opinion](https://www.txcourts.gov/supreme/orders-opinions/2026/may/may-15-2026/) and [official docket](https://dev-search.txcourts.gov/Case.aspx?cn=24-0250&coa=cossup) show **reversal and remand** concerning **application of the Texas Citizens Participation Act** to the fund's suit in response to the earlier Rule 202 petition. The Court did **not** reach a final merits decision upholding or striking down SB 8's constitutionality. The official docket also records **mandate June 26, 2026**. An interlocutory TCPA remand is not equivalent to a final statewide decision on substantive abortion restrictions.
5. [Texas Supreme Court — State v. Zurawski, May 31, 2024](https://www.txcourts.gov/supreme/orders-opinions/2024/may/may-31-2024/): historical judicial context **predating** the 2025 SB 31 amendments. The current guide correctly says later statutory text governs the newer medical-emergency clarifications instead of treating a 2024 opinion as the entire 2026 law.

## Result and limits

**No change required in the bounded facts checked.** The current authority guide clearly distinguishes emergency exceptions, the June 2025 changes, ectopic/miscarriage provisions, December 2025 abortion-drug law, several enforcement paths and the May 2026 case's limited procedural resolution. No unsupported description of a mandatory delay until active dying, a categorical ban on ectopic pregnancy treatment, identical liability of a pregnant woman and providers, or final *Weldon* merits resolution was detected in the reviewed paragraphs and FAQs. This is a neutral legal-process check and **not advice on pregnancy care, clinical decision-making or a person's legal exposure**.
