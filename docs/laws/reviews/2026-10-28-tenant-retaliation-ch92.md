# Texas residential tenant retaliation — bounded primary-source audit

**Checked:** October 28, 2026. **Authority:** [current Texas Property Code Chapter 92, Subchapter H, §§92.331–92.335](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PR/htm/PR.92.htm). **Guide:** `src/data/law-topic-landlord-tenant-authority.ts`.

## Statutory elements confirmed

- **§92.331(a):** Protected tenant conduct includes good-faith exercise of lease, municipal, federal or state rights; statutory repair notices; specified good-faith building/housing-code or utility complaints; and formation/participation in tenant organizations.
- **§92.331(b):** A landlord may not retaliate **within six months after the protected act** by filing an eviction except §92.332 valid grounds, unlawful exclusion, reducing services, increasing rent, terminating a lease, or material bad-faith interference with lease rights.
- **§92.332(a)–(b):** A landlord may prove action was not retaliatory; qualifying escalator clauses and uniform multidwelling project rent/service changes have specified exceptions; overdue rent and specified misconduct can provide independent lawful eviction/termination grounds. Never describe the six months as an automatic rent freeze or categorical eviction ban.
- **§92.333:** A tenant proving statutory retaliation can seek a **civil penalty of one month's rent plus $500**, actual damages, costs and reasonable attorney's fees, reduced by sums owed to the landlord; subsidized-rent cases apply the statute's fair-market-rent penalty rule.
- **§92.334:** A written no-code-violation / no-utility-problem determination in specified cases can create a rebuttable tenant bad-faith presumption, and bad-faith retaliatory-action suits have additional landlord remedies.
- **§92.335:** Retaliation under §92.331 is a defense to eviction; other Chapter 92 civil actions generally **cannot be joined or asserted as eviction crossclaims**.
