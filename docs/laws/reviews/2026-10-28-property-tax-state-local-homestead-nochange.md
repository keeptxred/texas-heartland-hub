# Texas property-tax policy: no state tax, local rate authority and enacted 2025 school-homestead measures

**Reviewed:** October 28–29, 2026. **Guide:** `src/data/law-topic-property-tax-authority.ts` / `/laws/topic/property-tax-law`. This is a **bounded statutory check**, not a complete taxpayer-rights or local-rate review. The guide retains its original `updated: "2026-09-18"` value.

## Official sources and independent verification

1. [Texas Comptroller — Property Tax Assistance](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/) and [Texas.gov Property Tax Transparency](https://www.texas.gov/living-in-texas/property-tax-transparency/) expressly state **Texas has no state property tax**. Local elected taxing-unit boards/councils set and collect property-tax rates under state legal constraints; the Comptroller does not determine an individual municipality's or school district's annual adopted rate. Appraisal districts assess property values, a separate function. The guide accurately makes this distinction and does not claim the state directly levies local property tax.
2. [Texas Tax Code §11.13(b)–(c), current Legislature text](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/TX/htm/TX.11.htm): the mandatory general **school district** homestead exemption is **$140,000** of qualifying appraised homestead value; an eligible person 65 or older or disabled is entitled to an **additional $60,000** school-district exemption. The dollar amounts do not automatically lower every city, county or district rate or apply to every nonhomestead property.
3. [Texas Constitution Article VIII, §1-b(c), current text](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/CN/htm/CN.8.htm) authorizes the $140,000 general school exemption and an additional qualifying older/disabled exemption up to $60,000.
4. [Official Legislative Reference Library, November 4, 2025 constitutional amendment election](https://lrl.texas.gov/legis/constamends/results.cfm?electionDate=2025-11-04): voter-adopted **Proposition 13 / SJR2** increased the general school homestead exemption from $100,000 to $140,000; **Proposition 11 / SJR85** separately authorized expansion of the additional school exemption for qualifying homeowners 65+ or disabled, implemented in Tax Code §11.13(c). The existing guide's assertion that both operated for tax year 2025 is consistent with enacted statutes and applicability provisions.
5. [Comptroller — Property Tax Exemptions](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/exemptions/) independently confirms school exemptions, residence/ownership eligibility, special local options and different treatment of age/disability provisions. [Comptroller — Truth in Taxation](https://comptroller.texas.gov/taxes/property-tax/truth-in-taxation/) separately explains no-new-revenue/voter-approval rate calculations; those are **not** interchangeable with adopted rates or an owner's final bill.

## Outcome and boundaries

**No on-page legal correction warranted within this checked claim set.** The current KTR authority guide accurately separates statewide statutory/constitutional policy from local tax-rate setting, distinguishes Proposition 11 versus 13 and school-taxable-value benefits from all-local-rate cuts, and intentionally links practical exemption applications, appraisal protests, calculators and property-tax administration to TexasDefined. That editorial scope prevents duplicative low-value pages and is retained.
