# Texas Administrative Procedure Act emergency rulemaking and petitions — bounded source review

**Date:** October 29, 2026. **Module:** `src/data/law-topic-administrative-rulemaking-authority.ts`. **Status:** Partial, not full legal claim sign-off.

## Current enacted primary law

[Texas Government Code Chapter 2001](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/GV/htm/GV.2001.htm):

- **§2001.034(a)–(b):** State agency can omit/abbreviate the ordinary prior notice/hearing only with a finding that imminent peril to public health/safety/welfare or a state/federal law requirement demands a rule on fewer than 30 days' notice, and must explain reasons in writing and emergency-rule preamble.
- **§2001.034(c)–(d):** Emergency rule effective **no more than 120 days**, renewable once for **no more than 60 days**; filed with SOS for Texas Register publication; identical rule may be adopted separately via ordinary §§2001.023/.029 procedures. Do not infer permanence from emergency label.
- **§2001.021(a)–(d):** Defined interested Texas resident, Texas-located business, qualifying government subdivisions or organizations can petition under the agency's prescribed procedure. Agency must **deny in writing with reasons or initiate rulemaking within 60 days** after submission. Starting rulemaking does not compel adoption of the requested final text.
