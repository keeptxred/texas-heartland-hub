# Texas Penal Code §9.43 — third-person property force (bounded review)

**Checked:** 2026-10-27. [Master issue #2938](https://github.com/keeptxred/texas-heartland-hub/issues/2938).

**Scope:** Use of force to protect the *land or tangible movable property of a third person*, not the entire Penal Code Chapter 9 or all self-defense claims. Original guide `updated=2026-09-06` remains accurate to its prior review record and is not advanced.

**Primary law:** [Texas Penal Code §§9.41–9.43](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PE/htm/PE.9.htm). Section 9.43 requires two distinct predicates:

1. Under the facts as reasonably believed, the person would be justified under §9.41 (ordinary force) or §9.42 (deadly force) as if protecting **their own** land or tangible movable property.
2. The actor reasonably believes either that the interference is attempted/completed theft of or criminal mischief to tangible movable property **or** the third person requested protection, the actor has a legal duty, or the third person is the actor's spouse/parent/child, lives with or is under the actor's care.

**Correction:** Previous guide named §9.43 without spelling out these conditions. Add specific framework, rule, FAQ and primary citation. Preserve §9.42's separate stricter immediate necessity, qualified offense, no-other-way / substantial-risk conditions for deadly force. No promise that a neighbor's property dispute justifies armed force or that a general 'Castle Doctrine' slogan applies.

**Remaining audit:** all remaining §9.31/§9.32 presumptions, special defenses, §9.33, §9.44 devices, law enforcement provisions, civil immunity and every other assertion/source still require independent review before claiming full-guide certification. No changes to original date or unrelated site controls.
