# Texas DTPA private consumer standing and conditional remedies — bounded source review

**Reviewed:** October 29, 2026. **Scope:** `src/data/law-topic-consumer-protection-authority.ts` only; targeted framework, rules and two new FAQs. The guide remains **Partial**, not a certification of every DTPA claim or factual scenario.

## Primary current authority

- [Official Texas Business & Commerce Code Chapter 17, §§17.45, 17.49, 17.50](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/Docs/bc/htm/bc.17.htm) (statute text current through the 89th Legislature's 2025 sessions; read October 29, 2026).
- §17.45(4) defines eligible consumers for goods/services purchased or leased and excludes business consumers with assets of **$25 million or more** or business consumers controlled or owned by an entity with that level of assets. A company buyer may otherwise be eligible; it is not restricted to individuals.
- §17.50(a)(1) requires **detrimental reliance** on a §17.46(b) specifically enumerated deceptive practice and **producing cause** of covered damages. §§17.50(a)(2)–(4) cover different types of private claims; do not copy reliance requirements blindly to every warranty or unconscionability allegation.
- §17.49(c),(f),(g),(i) contains professional-service and high-consideration transaction exclusions with material exceptions; avoid an absolute "professionals cannot be sued" or "$100,000 transactions are excluded" claim.
- §17.50(b)(1) allows proven economic damages for a prevailing consumer, mental-anguish damages following a **knowingly** or **intentionally** finding, and discretionary additional damages within the statutory different three-times formulas. Fees and costs are distinct under §17.50(c)–(d), including court finding for groundless, bad-faith or harassing suit.
- The preexisting 60-day notice and limitation statements reviewed October 28 were not rewritten; other statutory exemptions and remedies remain for full audit.
