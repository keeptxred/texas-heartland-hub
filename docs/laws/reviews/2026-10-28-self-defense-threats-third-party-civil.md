# Texas self-defense: threats, reckless injury to bystanders and civil consequences

**Reviewed:** October 28–29, 2026. **Guide:** `src/data/law-topic-self-defense-authority.ts` / `/laws/topic/self-defense-use-of-force`. **Scope:** statutory Chapter 9 §§9.04–9.06 and Civil Practice and Remedies Code §83.001, not a complete Chapter 9 case-law review.

## Primary-source legal findings

- [Current Texas Penal Code Chapter 9, official Legislature text](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/PE/htm/PE.9.htm):
  - **§9.04 — threats as justifiable force.** A threat is justified when the underlying **use of force** would be justified under Chapter 9. Threatening death or serious injury, including producing a weapon, **when purpose is limited to creating apprehension that deadly force will be used if necessary**, does not by itself constitute actually *using* deadly force. The statute does not legalize threatening someone over an insult or menace unsupported by a Chapter 9 force justification.
  - **§9.05 — reckless injury of an innocent third person.** Even where Chapter 9 justifies threatening or using force toward one person, it **does not** supply justification in a prosecution for **recklessly injuring or killing an innocent third person** as a result. Do not conflate the original target and third-party reckless harm.
  - **§9.06 — civil remedies unaffected.** Criminal justification under the chapter does not, merely by existing, abolish an otherwise available civil remedy. This must be distinguished from conditional statutory civil immunity and from whether a defense has actually been proven.
- [Texas Civil Practice and Remedies Code §83.001](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/GetStatute.aspx?Code=CP&Value=83.001): the defendant is immune from civil liability for **personal injury or death** caused by force or deadly force **that is justified under Chapter 9**. It is not a blanket shield based only on the actor's assertion of self-defense, an acquittal, or weapon-carry eligibility.
- The guide already cites [Penal Code Chapters 9.31–9.43](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/Docs/PE/htm/PE.9.htm) and CPRC Chapter 83, and correctly distinguishes deadly force, property protection, retreat and civil consequences. The added nuances explain important missing operative sections without changing existing true statements.
