# Bounded Texas divorce residence, jurisdiction and waiting-period source review

**Date:** October 29, 2026. **Guide:** `src/data/law-topic-family-custody-authority.ts`. **Status:** Partial — not a complete family-law certification.

## Current official authority

[Texas Family Code Chapter 6](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/FA/htm/FA.6.htm), available in the [official 2026 statutory compilation](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/sdocs/familycode.pdf):

- **§6.301**: when a suit for divorce is filed, **either petitioner or respondent** ordinarily must have been a Texas domiciliary for the preceding six months **and** a filing-county resident for the preceding 90 days. It is wrong to require both spouses to meet the duration.
- **§6.302**: separately addresses divorce filing by a nonresident spouse in the county where a qualifying Texas-domiciled spouse resides.
- **§6.303**: provides specified residence credit during absence on public service or accompanying a spouse. **§6.304**: treats certain service members stationed in Texas and their qualifying spouses as residents even if not previously residents. These are fact-specific and not a blanket exemption for every absent or new resident.
- **§6.702**: ordinary waiting time until the 60th day after the divorce suit was filed, with the particular family-violence finding exceptions in (c) and different treatment for annulment/void-marriage declarations under (b). Existing guide accurately describes that waiting rule. Residency eligibility is not a 60-day calendar period and neither divorce rule decides UCCJEA/custody jurisdiction by itself.
