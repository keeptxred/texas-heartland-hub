# Texas Payday Law — final pay, wage deductions, paid leave (bounded source review)

**Reviewed:** October 28–29, 2026. **Guide:** `src/data/law-topic-employment-workplace-authority.ts` / `/laws/topic/employment-wages-workplace-rights-law`.

## Current official sources and findings

- [Texas Labor Code Chapter 61, official consolidated code](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/LA/htm/LA.61.htm):
  - **§61.014(a)–(b):** An employee discharged by an employer generally must be paid in full no later than six days after discharge; other separations no later than next regular payday. Need to classify separation and identify compensation due. Existing guide accurately stated the main rule.
  - **§61.018(1)–(3):** Wage deductions/withholding require a court order, specific state/federal legal authority, or employee **written authorization** for a **lawful purpose**. An employer assertion that money is owed is not a fourth authorization type.
  - **§61.001(7)(B):** Wage definition includes vacation, holiday, sick, parental leave, severance and other fringe benefits that become payable under a written employer policy or agreement; entitlement and payment dates depend on terms.
  - **§61.015:** Commission and bonus timing may be determined by agreement/collective bargaining; payment still follows applicable Chapter 61 rules.
- [TWC: Deduction Problems under the Texas Payday Law](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/deduction_problems_under_tpl.html) and [Deduction Summary](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/texas_payday_law_deduction_summary.html): taxes and valid legal garnishment are distinct from employee-authorized voluntary deductions; even a signed deduction cannot override federal wage-hour limits. Written authorization must support an otherwise lawful deduction.
- [TWC: Final Pay](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/final_pay.html): ordinary final wages due within six **calendar** days after employer-initiated departure or on next regular payday after voluntary resignation. The employer may not postpone the final paycheck for missing equipment, unsigned timesheets or similar reasons. TWC says distinct earned commissions/fringe benefits can have schedules specified in compensation policy/plan.
- [TWC: Accrued Leave Payouts](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/accrued_leave_payouts.html) and [Vacation and Sick Leave](https://efte.twc.texas.gov/vacation_and_sick_leave.html): Texas does **not** require universal payout of unused vacation, sick leave or PTO; a written policy/agreement governs a qualifying entitlement.
- [Texas Workforce Commission administrative rule PDF, Chapter 821](https://www.twc.texas.gov/sites/default/files/ogc/docs/rules-chapter-821-payday-twc.pdf), **40 TAC §821.25(a),(g)**: vacation, sick leave, PTO/PDO cash-out on separation under the Texas Payday Law depends on a written agreement or policy specifically providing the payout. §821.25(g) has a particular PTO/PDO classification rule that should not be confused with a blanket cash-out requirement.
