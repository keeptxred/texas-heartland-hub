# Texas local government: home-rule charters and county delegated authority — bounded no-change review

**Checked:** October 28–29, 2026. **Guide:** `src/data/law-topic-local-government-authority.ts` / `/laws/topic/local-government-authority`. Public guide `updated` remains **2026-09-06**.

## Official legal sources and verified narrow statements

1. [Texas Constitution, Article XI, §5, current official text](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/GetStatute.aspx?Code=CN&Value=11.5):
   - Cities **having more than 5,000 inhabitants** may **adopt or amend** a home-rule charter by majority vote of qualified voters at an election. That population threshold does not itself automatically establish home-rule status.
   - A city that already adopted/amended its charter under the section and later falls to 5,000 or fewer **can still amend** that charter by majority vote; population decline does not, by itself, automatically revoke the charter.
   - The charter and ordinances remain subject to state and federal laws and applicable constitutional limits.
2. [Texas Local Government Code §5.004, current official text](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?artSec=5.004&chapter=LG.5&code=LG&tab=1): classification as a home-rule municipality depends on **operating under an adopted or amended home-rule charter**, not simply city size.
3. [Texas Local Government Code §51.072, current official text](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/LG/htm/LG.51.htm): home-rule municipality has full local-self-government power subject to superior authority, in contrast with general-law municipalities' statutory delegation. The guide correctly avoids describing home rule as absolute immunity from state preemption.
4. [Texas Health and Safety Code §826.014, official current text](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/docs/HS/htm/HS.826.htm): county commissioners courts may adopt local rabies-control ordinances/rules that meet specified public-health standards. It is **subject-limited authority**, not unrestricted general police power.
5. [Texas Attorney General Opinion KP-0491 (2025), official opinion summary](https://www.texasattorneygeneral.gov/opinions/ken-paxton/kp-0491): a county commissioners court **lacks authority** to penalize excessive barking as a separate general public nuisance without supporting statutory grant. The opinion distinguishes delegated rabies control from an expanded dog-barking nuisance penalty. This is an AG opinion, not a substitute for a controlling judicial decision.
