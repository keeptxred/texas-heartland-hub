# Texas Public Information Act §552.302 — bounded missed-ruling-deadline source review

**Date:** October 29, 2026. **Guide:** `src/data/law-topic-open-records-authority.ts`. **Full-guide review status:** Partial.

## Verified controlling law

- [Current Texas Government Code §§552.301–.302](https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/GV/htm/GV.552.htm). A governmental body generally must request an AG decision and inform the requester within the separate ten-business-day deadline, and submit supporting information/copy of arguments within fifteen business days when required under §552.301.
- **§552.302** expressly establishes a presumption that information requested in writing is subject to required disclosure if the government fails required ruling request, notice or argument-copy steps. Crucially this is not an unconditional release mandate: the text allows withholding for a **compelling reason**.
- [Office of the Attorney General — 2026 Public Information Handbook](https://oag.state.tx.us/sites/default/files/files/divisions/open-government/PIA-Handbook-2026.pdf), discussion of §§552.301-.302 and Texas Supreme Court `Paxton v. City of Dallas` (2017): material confidentiality and certain protected privileges can overcome a missed-deadline presumption. Do not invent a blanket loss of all exceptions or a universal ten-day physical-production deadline.
